How drawing works

Shapes are sampled onto the existing grid, so the highlight while you drag is the set of cells that will actually change — not the outline you drew. Editing the geometry stops the run and rebuilds the field from the scenario's initial condition: a domain that changes shape mid-flow is a moving-boundary problem this fixed-grid solver does not model.

Bands around the field show where each condition is applied, drawn from the same compiled data the solver reads. Flux is measured from the velocity field, positive into the domain; the net must be zero for an incompressible domain.

Why the speed is a control

The brush takes its DIRECTION from the drag, which the canvas mapping gives exactly, and its SPEED from this control. Pointer events are wall-clock and the fluid runs in simulated seconds, and the ratio between them varies by more than thirty times across these scenarios — so "the fluid moves with your hand" needs an invented constant, and this asks for it instead of hiding it. The dye value is carried only by placed sources: a brush stroke leaves nothing behind.

What these numbers are

The declared Reynolds number is an input: each scenario picks it along with a reference speed and length and derives ν from them, and a test asserts the three stay consistent. The peak figure uses the same length with the fastest fluid actually present. Shear rate is du/dy + dv/dx — vorticity is the same two derivatives subtracted. Wall shear is computed per face, where the normal is exact; the total force on a body is withheld, because summing over surface faces sums the staircase perimeter, which is 4/π times the true perimeter of a curved body at every resolution and does not converge. Rotation is counted where it exceeds strain by a stated margin rather than where Q > 0, which is a coin flip in a shear flow.

What changing the fluid does

Each scenario as shipped is water at 20 °C moving at 1 cm/s, in an apparatus sized so that reproduces it exactly. Another fluid fills the same apparatus. Where the speeds at the boundary are prescribed, the fluid reaches the velocity only through ν = μ/ρ, and a denser fluid of the same ν carries proportionally more pressure — that is incompressible flow, not a shortcut. Where a pressure difference is prescribed, the flow rate goes as 1/μ. A fluid that would put the grid past a cell Reynolds number of 20 — finer than anything here has been checked at — is refused rather than run.

What a probe reports

A probe reports the CELL under it, not an interpolated point: u and v are averaged from that cell's own faces, vorticity from its four corners. Pressure is shown with its datum named — with no pressure boundary anywhere the solution is only defined up to a constant, so the value at one cell means nothing and the difference between two means everything. "cell Re" is |u|h/ν, a property of this cell and the mesh; it is not the scenario's Reynolds number. Samples are taken once per solver step, and anything that rebuilds the field clears the history rather than joining two runs into one curve.
